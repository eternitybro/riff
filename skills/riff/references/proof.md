# Design proofs

A proof makes the reasoning inspectable. It does not replace usability testing or turn a plausible analogy into a measured result.

## Correspondence check

Mark each dimension **same**, **partly**, **different**, or **unknown**. Count only "same" in the match total and explain every judgment.

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Job | What is the person trying to accomplish? |
| Actors | Who acts, waits, or approves? |
| Choice set | How many options are there, and how similar are they? |
| Steps | What happens between starting and finishing? |
| Stakes | What does a wrong choice cost, and can it be undone? |
| Frequency | How often does the person do this? |
| Context | What device, attention, and surrounding activity are involved? |

Report, for example, "5 of 7 same; 1 partly; 1 unknown." Add:

- **Same because:** the matching dimensions and what they allow the design to borrow.
- **Different because:** the differences or unknowns and what should not transfer without further evidence.

Use five matches as a starting filter, not a validated score. Critical differences in stakes, accessibility, or user context may rule out an otherwise strong match.

## Proof beside each take

State the jobs and constraints, then the claim the take aims to support. Keep the argument to three to six rows.

This example is fictional and contains assumptions, not product findings:

| Statement | Reason |
|---|---|
| The user needs to compare two drafts before choosing. | Assumption for this example; confirm in the brief. |
| The comparison keeps the same content visible in both drafts. | Observable property of the proposed mock. |
| Keeping context stable may reduce the effort of comparing changes. | Hypothesis; requires testing with representative users. |
| The take therefore prioritizes comparison over browsing many drafts. | Design choice derived from the job and hypothesis above. |

In a real riff, cite code paths, supplied evidence, checked analogs, explicit jobs, or prior decisions. Label unsupported statements as assumptions. List gaps after the table, including what evidence would change the recommendation. Do not invent conversion rates, task-time savings, or usability improvements.

## Decision log

Record the chosen take or combination, date, supporting proof, accepted assumptions, and each rejected take's decisive tradeoff or gap. Record changes in scope explicitly so the next round can build on the decision.

Separate the agent's working assumptions from assumptions the user has accepted. Until the user chooses, record the recommendation as a recommendation and leave the pick pending.
